<CaseSection number="01" title="The problem">

Pacific EcoSolutions was a new agency offering AI-powered web and digital services to clients in the United States. Its core idea was an AI agent that would help each client identify their needs and find solutions, adapting to them instead of offering a fixed menu of services. The landing page had to present the agency and that idea.

</CaseSection>

<CaseSection number="02" title="My role">

I worked on the venture as an employee-partner and designed the landing page end to end in Figma: UX, page structure, content flow and visual design, for desktop (1440) and mobile (390). I started from a basic UI kit so I didn’t begin from zero, then redesigned most of it.

</CaseSection>

<CaseSection number="03" title="Process">

The kit gave me a generic agency layout. I rebuilt the page around the one idea that made this agency different, the AI agent, and organized everything else to support it: a hero, a five-step “How it works”, the services, a section to talk to the agent, who we are, mission and vision, and core values. I designed desktop and mobile versions, with illustrations from Storyset to keep a consistent, friendly visual language.

</CaseSection>

<CaseSection number="04" title="Key decisions">
  <ol class="list-decimal space-y-4 pl-6 marker:font-mono marker:text-ink-subtle">
    <li>
      <strong>The AI agent is the process, not an add-on.</strong> In “How it
      works”, step 2, Discovery, is where the agent analyzes the client’s needs.
      The business idea becomes the path the visitor reads, from identifying
      needs to delivery.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>“Talk to our AI agent” as the main moment.</strong> A voice-first
      interface (an animated orb, the question “How can AI help my business?”
      and a “Listening…” state), with the option to switch to chat. It’s the
      strongest call to action on the page.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>A dark section for the agent.</strong> In contrast with the light
      page, the agent section reads as the product moment, not as one more
      block.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>From strategy to delivery.</strong> Services are presented as one
      end-to-end path (AI business integration, AI agents, prototypes and MVPs,
      web development) instead of a loose list.
    </li>
  </ol>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection number="05" title="Honest result">

This is a design only. The venture didn’t move past the design stage and the site was never launched, so there are no users, traffic or results. What the case shows is how I turn a generic kit into a page built around one clear idea, and how I design an AI interaction (its states and the switch between voice and chat) before any code exists.

</CaseSection>

<CaseSection number="06" title="What’s pending">

If the venture resumed, the next steps would be:

  <ul class="list-disc space-y-2 pl-6 marker:text-ink-subtle">
    <li>Building the site and the AI agent behind it.</li>
    <li>Designing the agent’s conversation, including what happens when it can’t help.</li>
    <li>Testing the page and the agent with real prospective clients.</li>
  </ul>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection number="07" title="What I learned">
  <ul class="list-disc space-y-4 pl-6 marker:text-ink-subtle">
    <li>
      <strong>A UI kit is a starting point, not a design.</strong> The value was
      in reorganizing the page around one idea.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>Make the core idea the structure.</strong> When the AI agent
      became the process, the page didn’t need to explain it twice.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>Designing for AI means designing states.</strong> Listening,
      thinking, and switching to chat are part of the interface, not details for
      later.
    </li>
  </ul>
</CaseSection>
